\section{Research gaps}
Four gaps guide our analysis. First, an SAE's sparsity and reconstruction fidelity do not guarantee that a nearest text label correctly describes its feature: vocabulary coverage and the image--text geometry constrain naming, and neither naming nor decoder directions are checked against independent training seeds, so the semantic stability of a discovered concept is not established. Second, the pipeline depends on external resources at every stage that turns raw activations into a clinical claim -- an ontology-derived vocabulary, a generative model for phrase expansion, and one or more large language models as semantic judges -- so biases or errors in any of these components can be mistaken for properties of the discovered representation itself. Third, a report is selective evidence. An unmentioned concept may be visible, absent, or incorrectly named, and an external judge can additionally confuse omission with contradiction. Fourth, independently assigned labels omit relationships between concepts. We examine whether semantic clustering produces useful groups, while recognizing that embedding similarity does not establish an anatomical hierarchy or causal relation. Together, these gaps point to the broader difficulty of assessing interpretability in a scalable and reproducible way: our human and multi-model audit addresses evaluator reliability specifically, but it does not establish image-level clinical correctness or resolve the other three gaps.

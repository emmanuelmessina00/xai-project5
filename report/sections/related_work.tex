\section{Related work}
\textbf{From predefined concepts to discovery.} TCAV measures prediction sensitivity along directions learned from examples of user-defined concepts~\cite{kim2018tcav}. Concept Bottleneck Models instead predict annotated concepts as an intermediate representation and permit interventions before the final prediction~\cite{koh2020cbm}. Both offer semantic interfaces, but rely on a supplied concept specification. Bricken et al.~\cite{bricken2023monosemanticity} investigate sparse dictionary learning as a way to decompose polysemantic language-model activations. Discover-then-Name applies SAE discovery before naming concepts and training downstream concept bottlenecks~\cite{rao2024discover}. SpLiCE provides a complementary approach, decomposing CLIP embeddings into sparse combinations of textual concepts without training a new representation~\cite{bhalla2024splice}.

\textbf{VLMs and medical grounding.} Pach et al.~\cite{pach2025sae} evaluate SAE features in VLMs using a human-study benchmark and interventions, going beyond reconstruction alone. MedConcept~\cite{haque2026medconcept} combines sparse decomposition of Merlin CT embeddings, an ontology-derived vocabulary, cosine-based naming, and frozen MedGemma judgments of concept--report consistency. We retain this separation between discovery and evaluation, while changing the image domain, backbone, vocabulary, and activation selection.

\textbf{Evaluating explanations.} Reconstruction measures preservation of embeddings; human inspection assesses semantic coherence; interventions probe behavioral effects; report entailment measures textual support. These criteria are complementary. LLM judging scales more easily than manual review, but position bias has been demonstrated in comparative judging settings~\cite{shi2025judging}. This motivates label permutation and independent validation here, without assuming that either eliminates evaluator error.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Data and vocabulary}
We use the frozen BiomedCLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder and PubMedBERT text encoder to obtain 512-dimensional, L2-normalized embeddings. The NIH Chest X-ray 10k subset supplies 7,500 training images and 2,500 validation images, using the provided train/test splits. From Open-I, filtering invalid frontal images and unusable reports leaves 3,666 image--report pairs. Reports concatenate \emph{Findings} and \emph{Impression}; evaluation removes anonymization placeholders and dates. SAE training uses no report text or disease labels.

We extract 116 distinct candidate terms from the Open-I \emph{Problems} field. Removing 15 device, procedure, and technical terms and merging domain-aligned terms yields 100 parent concepts. UMLS~\cite{bodenreider2004umls} supplies exact-match identifiers and English SNOMED CT/MeSH synonyms where available. Ninety concepts receive a CUI; ten retain their original term as a fallback. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite generates five radiology-style phrases per concept. Independent text encoding produces $T\in\mathbb{R}^{998\times512}$: 498 terminology/fallback entries and 500 generated phrases. Thus, representation learning is unsupervised, but semantic naming uses curated external resources and evaluation-dataset metadata.

\subsection{Sparse decomposition and naming}
For an image embedding $x$, the SAE computes
\begin{equation}
z=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_e(x-b_d)+b_e),\qquad
\hat{x}=W_dz+b_d,
\end{equation}
with 1,024 hidden units. Its batch loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{512B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\|x_i-\hat{x}_i\|_2^2
+\frac{\lambda}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\|z_i\|_1,
\end{equation}
where $B=256$ and $\lambda=5\times10^{-5}$. Adam starts at $10^{-3}$; the learning rate halves after five epochs without sufficient validation improvement. Decoder columns are normalized after each update. Training allows 1,000 epochs, with early-stopping patience 25; the saved run stops at epoch 413 and selects epoch 388 by validation loss.

Each decoder column receives the phrase maximizing cosine similarity with its direction. Multiple units can share a label. For each image, positive activations are sorted and retained until their cumulative sum reaches 90\% of the positive activation mass; duplicate phrase labels are removed. All selected units are named, including those with low maximum similarity.

\subsection{Semantic evaluation and extensions}
Frozen MedGemma 1.5 4B receives only the proposed phrase and report. The few-shot prompt distinguishes \emph{Aligned} (explicit support), \emph{Unaligned} (explicit contradiction), and \emph{Uncertain} (omitted, ambiguous, or tentative). Three forward passes cycle the classes through single-token answers A/B/C. Softmax probabilities over these tokens are mapped back to classes and averaged before selecting the verdict. This avoids free-text parsing but does not guarantee correct judgments. For each image, we divide each verdict count by the number of unique predicted phrases, then average these proportions across images.

For structured analysis, each parent concept is represented by the normalized mean of its phrase embeddings. Agglomerative clustering uses cosine distance and average linkage. We evaluate silhouette scores for $k=3,\ldots,15$ and aggregate existing verdicts at $k\in\{3,5,8,10\}$ without querying the judge again. Cluster statistics count phrase--image pairs, rather than equally weighted images.

Finally, 25 pairs are sampled by the original verdict (8 Aligned, 8 Unaligned, 9 Uncertain). The three authors annotate reports and concepts with MedGemma labels hidden; majority vote defines the reference. Three commercial evaluators also label the same pairs through file attachment and pasted text. This is a small, stratified text audit, not an expert radiological assessment.

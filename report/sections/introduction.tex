\section{Introduction}
Medical vision-language models (VLMs) encode images and text in a shared representation and have achieved strong performance across clinical tasks. However, their high-dimensional, polysemantic latent representations make the clinical meaning of individual dimensions difficult to establish, limiting interpretability and trustworthiness in safety-critical applications. Concept-based explanations aim to express this representation through recognizable anatomy and findings. Following MedConcept~\cite{haque2026medconcept}, we study whether sparse autoencoder (SAE) features extracted without diagnostic supervision can be named and assessed against radiology reports.

We adapt the original 3D abdominal CT framework to 2D chest radiographs using BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2023biomedclip}. Our contribution is an implemented pipeline with phrase-level semantic grounding, an analysis of concept groups at several clustering resolutions, and an independent, multi-evaluator audit of the language-model judge that also surfaces the pipeline's dependence on external resources. The objective is to assess what the available evidence supports about interpretability, rather than to develop a diagnostic classifier. We find that report-based alignment is low on average but highly uneven across clinical subdomains, and that the choice of judge itself, not just the discovered concepts, is a major source of disagreement in how that alignment is measured.

\section{Introduction}
Medical vision-language models (VLMs) encode images and text in a shared representation, but the clinical meaning of individual latent dimensions is difficult to establish. Concept-based explanations aim to express this representation through recognizable anatomy and findings. Following MedConcept~\cite{haque2026medconcept}, we study whether sparse autoencoder (SAE) features extracted without diagnostic supervision can be named and assessed against radiology reports.

We adapt the original 3D abdominal CT framework to 2D chest radiographs using BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2023biomedclip}. Our contribution is an implemented pipeline with phrase-level semantic grounding, an analysis of concept groups at several clustering resolutions, and an independent audit of the language-model evaluator. The objective is to assess what the available evidence supports about interpretability, rather than to develop a diagnostic classifier.

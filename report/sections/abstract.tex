We investigate unsupervised concept discovery in medical vision-language models through a chest X-ray adaptation of MedConcept. A sparse autoencoder decomposes frozen BiomedCLIP embeddings into 1,024 latent features, named using 998 phrases associated with 100 clinical concepts. Training uses NIH images; semantic evaluation compares extracted concepts with Open-I reports using a frozen MedGemma evaluator. Across 500 images, mean per-image scores are 7.21\% Aligned, 22.49\% Unaligned, and 70.30\% Uncertain. We extend the analysis through hierarchical clustering and a blinded audit of 25 concept--report pairs. MedGemma agrees with the three authors' majority verdict on 56\% of audited pairs, compared with 68--76\% for the commercial evaluators tested. These results demonstrate a working concept-discovery pipeline while exposing the limitations of report-based evaluation: sparse reconstruction, textual agreement, and clinical validity are distinct properties.

We investigate unsupervised concept discovery in medical vision-language models through a chest X-ray adaptation of MedConcept. A sparse autoencoder decomposes frozen BiomedCLIP embeddings into 1,024 latent features, named using 998 phrases associated with 100 clinical concepts. We train on 7,500 NIH images~\cite{gronimo_nih10k}, then extract concepts from Open-I images~\cite{ykumards_openi} and evaluate them against the corresponding reports using a frozen MedGemma evaluator. Across 500 images, mean per-image scores are 7.21\% Aligned, 22.49\% Unaligned, and 70.30\% Uncertain. 
We further investigate alignment using hierarchical clustering, revealing substantial variation across clinical subdomains: the proportion of aligned concepts ranges up to 49.23\% across clusters. We complement this analysis with a blinded audit of 25 concept-report pairs.
MedGemma agrees with the three authors' majority verdict on 56\% of audited pairs, compared with 68-76\% for the commercial evaluators tested. These results demonstrate a working concept-discovery pipeline while exposing the limitations of report-based evaluation: sparse reconstruction, textual agreement, and clinical validity are distinct properties.

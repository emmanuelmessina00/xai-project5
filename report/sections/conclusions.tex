\section{Conclusions}
In this work, we adapted the MedConcept framework to 2D chest radiographs, establishing an end-to-end unsupervised pipeline that decomposes BioMedCLIP visual embeddings using a Sparse Autoencoder, grounds latent directions in a fine-grained PubMedBERT vocabulary, and evaluates semantic alignment against Open-I radiology reports. 

Our experimental findings demonstrate that while the SAE achieves near-lossless visual reconstruction ($MSE = 3.32 \times 10^{-5}$) under high sparsity ($L_0 = 44.0$), report-based semantic alignment remains low ($7.21\%$ Aligned), dominated by reporting omission bias ($70.30\%$ Uncertain). Hierarchical macro-clustering reveals that clinical agreement is highly localized, reaching $49.23\%$ Aligned on focal findings such as the costophrenic angle/sulcus.

Our human and multi-LLM audit exposes key methodological limitations. MedGemma 1.5 4B achieves only $56\%$ agreement with author consensus and suffers from negation parsing errors (e.g., misclassifying explicit negations as aligned), demonstrating that automated LLM judges cannot replace expert radiological review. Additional limitations include vocabulary reliance on Open-I metadata, unverified patient-disjoint NIH splits, and the lack of causal intervention tests. 

Ultimately, our work establishes that sparse reconstruction, report entailment, and clinical validity are distinct properties. Future research must combine unsupervised feature extraction with expert radiological oversight and intervention-based causal testing to achieve faithful medical VLM interpretability.

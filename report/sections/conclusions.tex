\section{Conclusions}
In this work, we presented an unsupervised concept discovery pipeline for 2D chest radiographs, adapting MedConcept through sparse autoencoders and report-based evaluation with MedGemma. The SAE achieves high embedding reconstruction fidelity ($\text{MSE} = 3.32 \times 10^{-5}$) with an average of 44 active units per image. Across 500 images and 12,996 concept--report pairs, mean per-image verdict percentages are $7.21\%$ Aligned and $70.30\%$ Uncertain. The predominance of Uncertain verdicts may partly reflect reporting omissions, since radiologists may leave normal anatomy or other visible features undocumented. Concept quality may also influence these results: noisy, redundant, or overly specific dictionary entries can complicate comparison with clinical reports.


Despite these textual limitations, hierarchical clustering reveals that visual alignment is highly domain-specific, with focal anatomical structures like the costophrenic angle reaching $49.23\%$ Aligned. The audit of 25 concept--report pairs also highlights the role of evaluator reliability. MedGemma agrees with author majority consensus on $56.0\%$ of pairs, compared with $68.0\%$--$76.0\%$ for the commercial models. These results suggest opportunities to improve automated evaluation, while a larger sample would help establish how consistently the observed differences hold.

Future research could refine the concept dictionary and validate feature-to-concept assignments to examine how these choices affect alignment. We also leave evaluation on a larger audit sample to future work. Expert review of the images could further assess whether the extracted concepts correspond to visible findings and influence model behavior.
